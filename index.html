      How to follow along on your phone

      Pretend portfolio, real instructions. You don't need to watch all day: get the pings, act when one comes in.

      $

      Leave blank to use the bot's own amounts. Fill it in and every trade shows your amount too (same percent of the account).

      Once Set up (5 minutes)
      1. Install ntfy (free, no account) from the App Store or Google Play.
      2. In ntfy, tap + and subscribe to this topic:
        Open in ntfy
      3. Stocks: use an app that lets you buy in dollars (fractional shares): Robinhood, Fidelity, Schwab, Webull, and others. Turn on dollar-based or fractional buying if it's a setting.
      4. Crypto: any app that sells Bitcoin, Ethereum and Solana, such as Coinbase or Kraken.
      5. Put this page on your home screen. iPhone: Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen. Android: Chrome → ⋮ → Add to Home screen.
      Buy When you get a BUY ping
      1. Tap the ping. This page opens. Look at Do this now.
      2. Check the "skip it if" price. If the price has already run past it, skip this one. Chasing a late entry is how the math goes bad.
      3. In your app: search the ticker → Buy → choose dollars → enter the amount → order type Market → submit.
      4. Protect it right away. Tap the stock → Sell → order type Stop (or "Stop loss") → enter the Stop price → all shares → submit. If your app won't allow a stop on fractional shares, set a price alert at the Stop price and sell when it rings.
      5. If the card shows a Target, set a price alert there too, and sell when it rings.
      Stocks are day trades: the bot sells them by 3:50 PM. If you can't check your phone near 3:50, either skip the trade or sell whenever you can that afternoon.
      Sell When you get a SELL ping
      1. Cancel the stop order or price alerts you set for that ticker (otherwise they could fire later).
      2. Sell all of it → order type Market → submit.
      If your stop order already sold it for you, you're done. Just cancel any leftover alerts.
      Move stop When you get a MOVE STOP ping
      1. The trade is going well, so the bot raises its safety net.
      2. Cancel your old stop order (or alert) → place a new one at the new Stop price.
      Oops Missed a ping?

      Open Holding right now. Anything listed there is still open, with its current Stop. Anything not listed has already been sold.

      Never act on an old BUY. If "Do this now" says it's older than 20 minutes, let it go and catch the next one.

      ABC What the words mean
      Market order
      Buy or sell right now at the current price.
      Stop (stop-loss)
      A price where you sell automatically to cap the loss.
      Target
      A price where you sell to take the profit.
      Trailing stop
      A stop that moves up as the price rises, but never moves down.
      Fractional shares
      Buying part of a share, so $25 can buy a slice of a $600 stock.
      Breakout
      The price pushes above a recent high, often the start of a move.
      VWAP
      The average price everyone paid today. Above it means buyers are in control.
      RSI
      A 0 to 100 momentum gauge. Under 30 means oversold, over 70 overheated.
      Volume
      How many shares traded. Big volume means a move has real interest behind it.

      The rules, in plain English

      Five rules, each based on published research. Stocks are bought and sold the same day. Crypto trades around the clock.

      1. Opening-Range Breakout (stocks)

      Watch the first 5 minutes after 9:30 AM. If buyers won that candle on heavy volume, buy when the price breaks above that candle's high and stays above VWAP. Sell by 3:50 PM.

      Zarattini & Aziz, "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?" (2023)

      2. VWAP Trend (SPY, QQQ)

      VWAP is the average price everyone paid today. Buy when the price is above it and the short-term trend is up; sell when a 5-minute candle closes back below it.

      Zarattini & Aziz, "VWAP: The Holy Grail for Day Trading Systems" (2023)

      3. Late-Day Momentum (SPY)

      If the first half hour of the day and the 3:00 to 3:30 stretch were both up, buy SPY around 3:30 and sell at 3:55. The last half hour tends to follow the first.

      Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, "Market Intraday Momentum," Journal of Financial Economics (2018)

      4. Crypto Breakout

      In an uptrend, buy when a 15-minute candle closes above the 24-hour high on big volume. A trailing stop follows the price up; sell within 24 hours.

      Bitcoin intraday time-series momentum research; Quantpedia, "Trend-following and Mean-reversion in Bitcoin" (2022)

      5. Crypto Dip-Buy

      In an uptrend, buy when the coin gets oversold (RSI under 30) and starts bouncing. Fixed profit target and stop; sell within 12 hours.

      Short-term mean reversion, same Bitcoin study

      Money rules

      Each trade risks about 1.5% of the account if its stop hits. A stock trade can use up to half the account, so two can run at once. Crypto is capped at 25% of the account, one coin at a time, so it never crowds out the stock trades. At most 3 trades open at once. Down 5% on the day means no new trades until tomorrow.

      Paper trades still pay realistic costs: 0.05% slippage each way on stocks; 0.40% fee plus 0.05% slippage each way on crypto.

      Good to know before you copy a trade

      • This is pretend money. Nothing here is financial advice, and past results don't predict future ones.
      • Most day traders lose money. In a study of nearly 20,000 Brazilian day traders who kept at it for 300+ days, 97% lost money (Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, 2019). An independent replication of the opening-range breakout found its edge mostly disappears after trading costs. That's why this page shows every loss and every fee.
      • Timing: on market days the bot checks every minute from 7:00 AM until just after the close. Nights and weekends it checks crypto less often. If you see a trade late and the price has run past the "skip it if" number, skip it.
      • Prices: stock prices come from CNBC (with Nasdaq as a backup) and crypto from Coinbase, via free public feeds.
      • Real accounts: brokers have their own rules for frequent day trading in small accounts. Check yours before trading real money.
